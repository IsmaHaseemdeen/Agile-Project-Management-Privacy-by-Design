
# Agile Project Management & Privacy by Design

## Project Overview

This project presents a critical academic analysis of **Agile Project Management and Privacy by Design**, using the FarmaY pharmaceutical ERP development case study.

The research examines how Agile Scrum was adopted to manage an inventory management system project in a regulated pharmaceutical environment, evaluating its suitability, implementation approach, positive practices, limitations, and lessons learned.

The study also investigates Privacy by Design principles, identifying privacy, access control, data integrity, and governance challenges associated with enterprise information systems.

**Project Type:** Academic Research & Project Management Case Study  
**Module:** Project Analysis and Practice  
**Project Scope:** Agile Scrum Evaluation, Project Governance, Stakeholder Analysis, Privacy by Design, and Critical Evaluation  
**Project Status:** Research and Critical Analysis Completed  
**Development:** No software application was developed.

---

## Case Study Background

The study examines FarmaY, a Brazilian pharmaceutical company that adopted Scrum to manage the development of an ERP inventory control module.

The project aimed to improve the management of pharmaceutical stock, particularly medicines approaching their expiry dates, to reduce potential inventory losses.

The original case study was documented by Azanha et al. (2017).

This repository presents an independent academic evaluation of the published case study. It does not represent direct involvement in managing or delivering FarmaY's actual ERP project.

The primary objectives of this analysis were to:

## 1. Agile Project Management Analysis

### 1.1 Agile Scrum Implementation

The case study investigated how FarmaY adopted the Scrum framework to manage changing requirements and support incremental project delivery.

The analysis considered:

- Product backlog creation and prioritisation
- Sprint planning and iterative delivery
- Daily Scrum meetings
- Sprint reviews and retrospectives
- Product Owner responsibilities
- Scrum Master responsibilities
- Team autonomy and collaboration
- Stakeholder feedback and requirement changes

### 1.2 Suitability of Scrum

Scrum was evaluated as an appropriate methodology for the FarmaY ERP project because of its changing requirements, operational complexity, and need for continuous stakeholder feedback.

The analysis found that iterative delivery supported adaptability and early validation.

However, the effectiveness of the methodology was constrained by organisational readiness, limitations in team autonomy, and challenges in managing changing requirements.

### 1.3 Good Project Management Practices

The study identified several positive practices:

**Iterative Delivery**

Time-boxed sprints supported incremental progress and earlier evaluation of project deliverables.

**Stakeholder Collaboration**

Continuous involvement of business stakeholders helped align project outcomes with operational requirements.

**Backlog Management**

Product backlog prioritisation enabled the project team to focus on business value and adapt to emerging requirements.

**Continuous Improvement**

Sprint reviews and retrospectives provided opportunities to evaluate completed work and improve future activities.

**Early Value Delivery**

Incremental releases allowed stakeholders to assess results earlier than would typically be possible with a fully sequential approach.

## 2. Project Management Challenges

The research also identified limitations that reduced the effectiveness of Scrum implementation.

| Identified Challenge | Impact on Project Management |
|---|---|
| Insufficient Product Owner preparation | Communication difficulties between technical and business stakeholders |
| Overreliance on Scrum Master | Reduced team autonomy and decision-making independence |
| Weak self-organisation | Increased dependence on central leadership |
| Uncontrolled requirement changes | Scope extension and scheduling delays |
| Traditional planning habits | Reduced adaptability and Agile maturity |
| External IT dependencies | Slower issue resolution and limited cross-functional independence |
| Limited formal monitoring | Reduced visibility into risks and project progress |

These findings demonstrate that adopting Scrum ceremonies alone does not guarantee effective Agile project management.

Successful implementation requires organisational readiness, well-defined responsibilities, empowered teams, and appropriate governance.

---

## 3. Lessons Learned and Recommendations

The research identified several lessons applicable to future projects.

### Strengthen Agile Role Readiness

Ensure Product Owners and Scrum Masters understand their responsibilities and possess appropriate business, technical, and facilitation skills.

### Improve Backlog Governance

Establish structured prioritisation and change-impact assessment to balance flexibility with project control.

### Encourage Team Autonomy

Support self-managing teams by reducing excessive dependence on individual leadership roles.

### Improve Organisational Alignment

Ensure supporting departments and external stakeholders are appropriately involved in Agile processes.

### Integrate Risk Monitoring

Introduce suitable progress-tracking, risk management, and review mechanisms to identify problems earlier.

### Promote Continuous Improvement

Use retrospective findings to develop action plans and improve collaboration, delivery practices, and team effectiveness.

These are research-based recommendations rather than activities personally implemented within FarmaY.

## 4. Privacy by Design Analysis

Privacy by Design (PbD) is an approach that integrates privacy considerations into systems, organisational processes, and project decisions from the beginning.

The research examined the relevance of this approach to the FarmaY ERP project.

### 4.1 Privacy and Data Protection Concerns

The assessment highlighted several issues:

- User access controls and permissions
- Data integrity and validation
- Audit trails and accountability
- Personal information associated with access logs
- Integration of privacy requirements during system design
- Coordination between technical teams and governance stakeholders

### 4.2 Challenges of Implementing Privacy by Design

**Late Integration of Privacy Controls**

Privacy and governance requirements may become more difficult and costly to address when they are considered after functional design decisions.

**Access Control Complexity**

Enterprise systems require appropriate access restrictions while supporting necessary business operations.

**Organisational Dependencies**

Privacy implementation requires coordination among business stakeholders, technical teams, and governance functions.

**Data Integrity and Auditability**

Enterprise systems need reliable records and suitable audit mechanisms to support accountability.

**Regulatory Considerations**

The report evaluates GDPR-related principles as part of its academic privacy analysis. This does not constitute a legal compliance audit of FarmaY.

## 5. Key Research Findings

The analysis concluded that:

1. Agile Scrum can support adaptability, stakeholder collaboration, and early value delivery in complex enterprise projects.
2. The success of Agile depends on effective implementation, organisational readiness, and team autonomy.
3. Poorly managed requirement changes and external dependencies can reduce delivery efficiency.
4. Governance and risk management remain essential even in flexible project environments.
5. Privacy by Design should be considered during early project planning and system design.
6. Access controls, auditability, data integrity, and accountability are important considerations in enterprise information systems.

Overall, the project demonstrates the importance of combining effective project management practices with appropriate privacy and governance considerations.
